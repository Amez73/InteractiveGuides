Journalists and media workers CPJ has confirmed were murdered, named individually

CPJ classifies a killing as murder when a journalist is deliberately targeted for their work, as distinct from being killed incidentally in crossfire or an area strike. As of 25 June 2026, CPJ names 75 such confirmed cases across Gaza, Lebanon, Iran and Yemen, 59 journalists and 16 other media workers. This list is copied directly from CPJ's own published roster, including names CPJ itself later removed after further investigation, shown struck through with the reason CPJ gave.

On named individuals. The purple entries are a different kind of record from the rest of the timeline: single, named people whose killings drew their own independent investigations, forensic, journalistic or judicial, rather than being counted only within a larger toll. They are not the only such cases. CPJ alone has documented and named over 260; this timeline includes six of the most extensively investigated, plus the aggregate CPJ count. Selecting which individual killings to include is itself a judgment call, made here on the basis of independent forensic or investigative documentation existing, not on any assessment of whose death mattered more.
What is still missing. This is 80 entries against a documented universe orders of magnitude larger. Categories were added in direct response to identified blind spots: protected-site destruction (hospitals, schools), slow-mechanism deaths (infrastructure collapse, detention, starvation), and demographic erasure (entire family lines killed). Each addition surfaced others not yet built out. Religious and cultural sites are one: 227 mosques and 3 churches were damaged or destroyed, per UN figures, and none has an individual entry here. West Bank settler violence is another: it operates through a different mechanism again, and it is not represented on this timeline at all. The hatched bands on the timeline mark condensed spans of low chip density, not periods without killing. Six entries were added specifically to populate the 1957–2019 stretch, but coverage there is still thin relative to 1948 or 2023–26:
  • 1947–56. Salman Abu Sitta's Atlas of Palestine lists 232 sites of atrocity, massacre, destruction or plunder. Badil's survey of UN and Israeli archives counts more than thirty documented massacres in 1948 alone; one Israeli historian's fuller archival review puts it above a hundred, ten of them major. Sixteen individual entries are here.
  • 1957–1967. A decade of cross-border shootings of Palestinians attempting to return or cross armistice lines, referenced in Israeli and UN records but never aggregated into a reliable toll. Nothing from this decade is on the timeline.
  • 1967 and after. The 1967 war's village demolitions and killings, and the 1971 Gaza campaign, are now represented, but a large share of the underlying archive remains sealed by Israel's own state archivist, so the tolls given are ranges and estimates, not counts.
  • 1993–2002. The Oslo years and the run-up to the Second Intifada carry no entries here; B'Tselem's continuous fatality database covers this period in full.
  • 2019–2023. Sporadic escalations, including May 2021 beyond the single al-Wehda Street strike already listed, are underrepresented.
  • The current war. Airwars and ACLED log thousands of separate civilian-harm events in Gaza since October 2023. The New Humanitarian's database alone tracks more than 500 incidents involving aid seekers. Twenty-one individual entries are here.
  • The West Bank is still substantially underrepresented outside the aggregate entries. B'Tselem's fatality database records Palestinians killed there continuously from 1987 to now, including 1,105 since October 2023, at least 242 of them children.
On the wording. The one-line descriptions use plain verbs, shot, shelled, burned, executed, rather than "murdered". Not to soften anything: several entries are contested, and a legal verdict used as a label makes the whole list dismissible on its weakest entry. Where a court, commission or inquiry did reach a finding, it is stated in the detail card, including the UN's 2025 genocide finding.
On counting children. Roughly half these entries carry no child figure because none was ever recorded. That is the record, not a gap in the research. Consistent child counts appear only once UNRWA rolls, health ministry registries and human rights fieldwork exist to produce them, which is to say, from the mid-twentieth century onward, and unevenly even then.
How this was built. Every figure comes from a UN body, the Red Cross, an Israeli or international human rights organisation, or an academic historian. Where sources conflict the range is given rather than averaged. Incidents with genuinely disputed attribution are excluded: the al-Ahli hospital blast of 17 October 2023, put at 471 dead by Gaza's health ministry, is left off because Israeli and US intelligence attributed it to a misfired Palestinian rocket.
Accountability, across 78 years. After the 1956 Kafr Qasim massacre, the soldiers convicted had every sentence commuted within three years, and the brigade commander was fined ten prutot, at the time a coin worth a small fraction of a cent. After the 1948 Hula massacre in Lebanon, one officer was convicted, given a one-year sentence he never served, and went on to run the Jewish Agency. After the 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacre, Defense Minister Ariel Sharon resigned, then later became prime minister. In 2025, one deputy commander was dismissed over the killing of a medic convoy. Everything else was investigated internally and closed, investigated externally and rejected, or never investigated at all.
