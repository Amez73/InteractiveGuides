Every conversation about Palestine starts in the wrong place — 1948, 1967, or October 7, depending on who's telling it — and each side accuses the other of omitting the context that changes everything. Context does change everything. So this guide starts further back, moves chronologically, and names what it sees.

One thing stated at the outset: this is not a conflict between two equal sides. One party has one of the most powerful militaries on earth, backed unconditionally by the world's dominant superpower. The other has been under occupation, siege, or dispossession for more than 75 years. That asymmetry is the starting point, not a conclusion.

The Frame: Settler Colonialism

Israel was built as a settler-colonial project, one designed not to rule the people already on the land but to replace them. Its own founders said so, in writing. Get this one idea and the rest of the story snaps into focus.

One concept unlocks everything: settler colonialism. Regular colonialism exploits — the British needed Indian labour. Settler colonialism replaces: the land is the target, and the people on it are an obstacle to be removed. North America is the most documented case; what happened to Native populations wasn't incidental to the settlement project. It was the project.

The defining logic of settler colonialism

Patrick Wolfe, the historian who formalised the concept: "Settler colonialism destroys to replace." Not a description of every individual settler's intent — a description of the structural logic of the project as a whole.

The frame applies directly. Zionism, founded in 1897 in response to European antisemitism, chose a land that was not empty: ~500,000 Palestinians lived there. Its own leaders wrote the problem down — Herzl's diary on the need to "spirit the penniless population across the border"; Jabotinsky, forefather of today's Likud, on the "iron wall" of military force behind which settlement must proceed. That is the movement's documented intellectual history, and holding it doesn't require denying the Holocaust or Jewish refugees' genuine need for safety.

Why the frame matters: A territorial dispute between equals calls for compromise. A settler-colonial project calls for a different analysis — one where an occupied people's resistance to occupation cannot be morally equated with the violence of the occupying power, and "both sides" becomes incoherent in a specific way. Fanon argued this in 1961. It remains the correct distinction.
Deep Dive: Why global capital backs a territorial settler project

If you've read Part 2, you may notice an apparent contradiction: modern imperialism is financial and abstract — debt, trade rules, capital flows. So why does global capital unconditionally back an intensely territorial settler project here?

Because financial imperialism requires physical anchors. The Eastern Mediterranean sits at the junction of three continents, beside the Suez Canal (12% of global trade), within striking distance of the world's largest proven oil reserves. Debt traps are enforceable because behind the spreadsheet sits credible military force. Israel functions as a subsidized geopolitical outpost: it projects force, protects supply chains, disciplines regional governments, and field-tests the weapons US defense contractors sell worldwide. Settler colonialism is the local mechanism. Imperial strategy is the reason it gets $3.8 billion a year.

Without this integration, Palestine looks like an isolated ethnic tragedy. It is not. It is what happens when the global extraction system needs a physical anchor in the most strategically valuable real estate on earth.

The History: Ottoman Palestine to the Nakba

Palestine was never empty. Over a few decades it was turned from an Arab-majority land into a state where the majority had been expelled or scattered: 750,000 people, 400+ villages destroyed, none ever allowed home. Here it is, year by year.

Deep Dive: The Mandate Era & Early Migration, how settlement worked before 1948

The First Zionist Congress met in Basle in 1897. Herzl founded the movement with the explicit goal of a Jewish state. Early settlers purchased land from absentee Ottoman landlords, often displacing Palestinian tenant farmers who had worked the land for generations. Tensions with Palestinian Arab residents began immediately over land, labour exclusion policies, and political organisation.

British Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour wrote to Lord Rothschild declaring British support for "the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people." The letter also stated this should not prejudice "the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities in Palestine." Britain had no legal authority over Palestine at this point; it was still Ottoman territory. The promise was made by one colonial power about land it didn't own, to a third party, about a population that was not asked.

After WWI, Britain received a League of Nations mandate over Palestine. Jewish immigration accelerated, partly driven by the Holocaust and European antisemitism. The 1936–39 Arab Revolt was a general strike and armed uprising against British rule and Zionist immigration, brutally suppressed by British forces with the assistance of Zionist paramilitary organisations.

What was proposed

Palestinians reject it: 33% of the population getting 56% of the territory, including the most productive land, without the consent of those who own most of it.

Deep Dive: 1948, the Nakba in documented detail

Before Israel's declaration of independence, Zionist military forces, primarily the Haganah and its elite unit the Palmach, began military operations in areas allocated to the Jewish state and beyond. What followed is documented by Israeli historians (Benny Morris, Ilan Pappé) using Israeli military archives.

On April 9, 1948, forces from the Irgun and Lehi attacked the village of Deir Yassin near Jerusalem. Between 107 and 120 villagers were killed, including women and children. The attack was documented by the International Red Cross. The news spread across Palestine, contributing to the mass flight of Palestinians from surrounding areas.

Plan Dalet. Declassified in the 1980s, this Haganah military plan specified operations to "cleanse" areas of Arab villages, with explicit provision for destroying villages and expelling populations. Historian Ilan Pappé uses it as evidence of planned ethnic cleansing. Benny Morris disputes the characterisation while acknowledging expulsion was the result.

For decades, Israel's official position characterised Palestinian displacement as voluntary: Palestinians left because Arab leaders told them to. The opening of Israeli military archives in the 1980s produced "New Historians," Israeli scholars who documented from internal records that the displacement was organised and in many areas explicitly ordered. The historical facts are not seriously contested among scholars.

Deep Dive: The Nakba

The Catastrophe: What Actually Happened

In under two years, around 85% of the Palestinians in what became Israel were expelled or fled, 400+ villages were bulldozed, and not one refugee was ever allowed home. Everything that followed sits on top of this.

Between November 1947 and the end of 1948, the demographic and physical landscape of Palestine was transformed in ways that permanently shaped everything that followed. Here is what the historical record, including Israeli military archives and the work of Israeli historians, shows.

The scale of displacement. Approximately 750,000 Palestinians, between 80 and 85% of the Arab population of what became Israel, left or were expelled from their homes. They fled to the West Bank, Gaza, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria, where they and their descendants remain as refugees to this day. The UN counts approximately 5.9 million registered Palestinian refugees globally as of 2023.

The villages. Palestinian historian Walid Khalidi documented 418 Palestinian villages that were depopulated during the Nakba. Of these, the majority were subsequently demolished: bulldozed, mined, or built over. Some were converted into Israeli communities or parks. The village of Deheisha became a refugee camp. The site of Lifta, on the outskirts of Jerusalem, stands partially intact and is one of the few remaining visible remnants of a pre-1948 Palestinian village.

Plan Dalet. Benny Morris disputes the characterisation of it as a blueprint for expulsion while acknowledging that expulsion was the result. The document exists; its content is not disputed.

The right of return. UN Resolution 194, passed in December 1948, states that Palestinian refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date. Israel has rejected this resolution consistently for more than 75 years. The right of return remains the central unresolved issue of the conflict, and the one whose resolution would most directly challenge the demographic composition of the Israeli state.

Why the Nakba is the foundation: Every subsequent development, the 1967 occupation, the settlement project, Gaza, October 7, is downstream of 1948. The Palestinian refugee population exists because of the Nakba. The territorial claims exist because of the Nakba. The resistance exists because of the Nakba. You cannot understand anything that has happened since without it.
Deep Dive: The Haavara Agreement

1933: The Deal That Funded the 1930s Settlement Boom

In August 1933 the Zionist Federation of Germany signed a transfer deal with Hitler's government. It moved roughly 60,000 German Jews and about $100 million in capital into Palestine before the war shut it down. It is the clearest documented answer to the question this guide keeps circling: when rescue and settlement pointed in different directions, which one did the movement's leadership choose?

What it was. Haavara means "transfer" in Hebrew. Nazi flight taxes stripped emigrating Jews of nearly everything they owned. Under the agreement, signed by the German Reich Ministry of Economics, the Zionist Federation of Germany, and the Jewish-owned Anglo-Palestine Bank, an emigrating Jew instead paid into a blocked account in Germany. That money bought German export goods, machinery, pipes, pumps, cement, chemicals, which were shipped to Palestine and sold there. The emigrant was reimbursed in Palestine currency on arrival, typically recovering somewhere between half and two thirds of what they put in. Nazi Germany got hard currency and a broken boycott. The emigrant got out with something rather than nothing.

Why it belongs in this guide. The 1930s are the decisive decade in Station 2's timeline. Between 1931 and 1939 the Jewish population of Palestine roughly tripled, and the capital arriving alongside those people built farms, factories, roads and institutions. Haavara was one of the largest single channels of that capital. The demographic and economic base that made 1948 possible was assembled in these years.

Now notice what the arrangement selected for. A movement organised around rescuing Jews takes any exit that saves lives: Britain, America, Argentina, anywhere the doors open. A movement organised around building a state in Palestine needs the exit that ends in Palestine specifically, and will deal with the persecutor to keep that one open. The mainstream leadership dealt. That is not a side effect of the agreement, it is the logic of it.

The fight over it. Jews argued about this bitterly at the time. The Jewish Agency's political department, led by Chaim Arlosoroff, negotiated and defended it: get people and their property out while the exit is still open. Ze'ev Jabotinsky's Revisionists and the organisers of the international anti-Nazi boycott called it a betrayal, a lifeline of hard currency handed to Berlin at the exact moment the world was trying to strangle it economically. Arlosoroff was shot dead on a Tel Aviv beach in June 1933, weeks before the agreement was finalised. The murder was never solved, and the accusations that followed ran straight through the transfer fight.

They said it out loud. Israeli historian Tom Segev documents David Ben-Gurion telling Mapai's central committee in December 1938 that if he could save all of Germany's Jewish children by sending them to England, or only half by sending them to Palestine, he would choose Palestine. Ben-Gurion was arguing a political line about where the movement should put its effort, not deciding anyone's fate. But the ranking is explicit, from the man who would declare the state ten years later: the destination mattered, not only the rescue.

Was it collaboration? Get this exactly right.

It was cooperation with the Nazi state. That is not a slur, it is the documented record: a signed agreement, six years of trade, mutual benefit. And the people who called it collaboration at the time were Jews. Jabotinsky's Revisionists and the anti-Nazi boycott organisers said that breaking a collective front against Hitler for sectional gain was a betrayal of Jews everywhere. They lost that argument. The side that won, the Labour Zionist mainstream and the Jewish Agency, went on to found the state.

What it shows is priority. Not that the movement's leaders wished Jews harm, but that when saving lives and settling Palestine came apart, they chose Palestine and paid the persecutor to make it possible.

What it does not show is complicity in genocide. Haavara ended in September 1939. The extermination decision came in 1941. Nothing about a 1933 shipping arrangement enabled or anticipated it, and the 60,000 people who used it were refugees saving their own lives with the only exit available to them. Anyone citing this agreement to argue that Jews took part in their own destruction is lying to you, and that lie is antisemitism wearing the costume of history.

What this is evidence about: A political movement's strategy. Nothing else. Zionism is a political project. Judaism is a faith. What the Jewish Agency decided in 1933 tells you about the priorities of a state-building movement, and it tells you nothing whatsoever about Jewish people, who were its intended beneficiaries and, in Germany, its desperate applicants. Keep those two things apart or you will end up somewhere ugly. The finding here is narrow and it is enough: presented with a choice between solidarity with Jews under attack everywhere and the accumulation of people and capital in one specific piece of land, the leadership chose the land. That is the logic running through the rest of this guide, showing up early and in writing.

Deep Dive: 1967, the Six-Day War & the beginning of occupation

In six days, Israel captured the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) from Jordan, the Gaza Strip and Sinai Peninsula from Egypt, and the Golan Heights from Syria. Israel established military administration over the West Bank and Gaza immediately.

The first Israeli settlements, civilian communities built on occupied Palestinian land, began in 1967, immediately after the war. The Fourth Geneva Convention prohibits an occupying power from transferring its civilian population into occupied territory. Israel has consistently rejected the applicability of this provision. The international legal consensus, including advisory opinions from the International Court of Justice, holds that the settlements are illegal.

Settlement construction accelerated under Likud governments. By 1993, over 100,000 Israeli settlers lived in the West Bank.

Deep Dive: The USS Liberty, June 8, 1967

On June 8, 1967, one day before Israel launched its assault on the Golan Heights, Israeli air and naval forces attacked the USS Liberty, an American signals intelligence ship operating in international waters off the Sinai coast. The attack lasted approximately 75 minutes. Israeli jets strafed the ship with cannon fire and napalm. Israeli torpedo boats hit it with a torpedo and then machine-gunned sailors in life rafts. 34 American sailors were killed and 171 were wounded.

Israel's official position, then and now, is that the attack was a tragic case of mistaken identity. The US government accepted this explanation. But a significant body of evidence points in a different direction.

What the evidence shows

The ship was clearly American. The Liberty was flying a large American flag and had "USS LIBERTY" painted on its hull in English. Surviving crew members testified that the flag was visible. Israel claimed its pilots could not identify the ship, but the attack lasted over an hour across multiple waves, with pilots close enough to strafe individual sailors on deck.

Israeli forces jammed American emergency frequencies. When the Liberty tried to radio for help, Israeli forces jammed the ship's emergency distress frequencies. Jamming requires knowing the specific frequencies in advance and deliberately targeting them. The Liberty's crew had to jury-rig an antenna to get a distress call out. This is not consistent with a case of mistaken identity.

NSA intercepts suggest Israeli pilots identified the ship as American. Declassified NSA documents confirm the Liberty was intercepting Israeli military communications at the time of the attack. Former NSA analysts have stated that intercepts captured Israeli pilots identifying the ship as American before and during the attack. The full NSA file remains partially classified.

The US Navy's own investigation was a cover-up. Captain Ward Boston, the senior legal counsel for the Navy's 1967 Court of Inquiry, signed a sworn declaration in 2004 stating that he and the court's president, Admiral Isaac Kidd, were ordered by President Johnson and Secretary of Defense McNamara to conclude the attack was an accident. Boston stated: "I am certain that the Israeli attack was a deliberate attempt to sink an American ship and murder its entire crew." Admiral Thomas Moorer, former Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, convened an independent commission in 2003 that concluded the attack was deliberate and that the US government covered it up.

The Golan Heights connection

Here is what is established: the Liberty was a SIGINT (signals intelligence) ship whose mission was to intercept communications in the region. Israel attacked Syria's Golan Heights on June 9, the day after the Liberty attack. The United States had been pressuring Israel not to open a front against Syria. The Liberty's surveillance equipment was capable of intercepting Israeli military communications, including operational planning.

The theory: Israel attacked the Liberty because it was intercepting Israeli communications that revealed plans to assault the Golan Heights. If the US learned of these plans through the Liberty's intercepts, it could have applied diplomatic pressure to stop the attack, or worse, warned Syria. Destroying the ship removed that risk.

How strong is this theory? The circumstantial evidence is substantial. The timing is exact: one day before the Golan assault. The motive is clear: the US opposed the Golan operation, and the Liberty could hear Israeli planning. The behavior is consistent: jamming frequencies, attacking life rafts, and sustaining the assault for over an hour all suggest the goal was to sink the ship and leave no witnesses. Former NSA officials and military intelligence officers have publicly stated they believe this was the reason.

What the theory lacks is a declassified Israeli document explicitly stating this intent. No such document has surfaced. Israel's internal communications about the decision to attack the Liberty remain classified. The US government has never conducted a full, independent investigation despite requests from survivors, members of Congress, and former military officials including the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs.

The honest assessment: The evidence that Israel deliberately attacked a clearly marked American ship is strong. Multiple US military officials with direct knowledge have said so under oath. The evidence that the specific motive was to clear the way for the Golan Heights assault is circumstantial but coherent: the timing, the motive, and the behavior all align. No competing explanation accounts for why Israel would deliberately attack its most important ally's ship on the eve of a military operation that ally opposed. The absence of a smoking-gun document is real, but it is also the predictable result of two governments that agreed to bury the incident.

What is not in dispute: 34 Americans were killed. The US government told their families it was an accident. No American official has ever been held accountable for suppressing the investigation. No Israeli official has ever been held accountable for the attack.

Deep Dive: The Oslo Accords, the gridlocked peace process

Signed on the White House lawn, the Oslo Accords created the Palestinian Authority, established the framework of a "two-state solution," and divided the West Bank into Areas A (Palestinian civil and security control), B (Palestinian civil, Israeli security), and C (full Israeli control, covering 60% of the West Bank).

Oslo was designed as a five-year transition to a Palestinian state. Settlement construction accelerated during and after Oslo; the settler population more than doubled. Critics argue Oslo functioned as Palestinian management, not liberation, requiring Palestinian leaders to police their own population on Israel's behalf while Israel continued colonising the land.

The Camp David negotiations in 2000 collapsed, and no subsequent framework has come closer to producing a Palestinian state. The Oslo framework remains the nominal basis of international diplomacy while the situation on the ground has moved steadily in the opposite direction.

The claim that Israel "ended the occupation" by withdrawing settlers in 2005, and therefore bears no responsibility for Gaza, collapses on the facts. After 2005 Israel retained control of Gaza's airspace, territorial waters, population registry, electromagnetic spectrum, and six of seven land crossings, and from 2007 imposed a comprehensive blockade on the movement of people and goods.

Under international law, that is occupation by remote control. The UN, the International Committee of the Red Cross, and human rights organisations continued to classify Gaza as occupied throughout, describing it as the world's largest open-air prison. "We left" was a public-relations claim, not a legal or physical reality.

Netanyahu retracted it, conceding "the Nazis decided on that by themselves." The episode is instructive: a sitting Israeli prime minister rewrote the history of the Holocaust itself in order to assign its authorship to Palestinians.

The Founding Myths, and What the Record Shows

Every settler-colonial project needs a mythology. Israel's rests on ten foundational stories, repeated so often they function as common sense. Historian Ilan Pappé dismantled each one, using Israel's own archives. Here they are, with the evidence that collapses them.

Source: The myths below are drawn from Ilan Pappé's chapter "The Ten Mythologies of Israel" in Gaza in Crisis (Noam Chomsky & Ilan Pappé, 2010). Pappé is an Israeli historian, professor at the University of Exeter, and one of the "New Historians" whose work in Israel's own declassified military archives rewrote the accepted narrative of 1948. The analysis below adapts his arguments with additional context.

Tap any myth to see the reality behind it.

Myth 1: "Palestine was a land without a people for a people without a land" The founding slogan. Palestine had roughly 1.3 million inhabitants, a thriving society with cities, farms, trade networks, and a distinct cultural identity.

Palestine was not empty. Before Zionism arrived, Palestine had a thriving society, mostly rural, with established urban centres, under Ottoman rule, with land ownership patterns, trade networks, schools, and a distinct Palestinian Arab cultural identity. It would have become a nation-state like Iraq or Syria had Zionism not arrived on its shores.

The second part, a supposed unbroken genetic link between Zionist settlers and the Jews of Roman-era Palestine, is doubtful, but beside the point. Every national movement invents an origin story. What matters is what you do in its name. In this case: colonise, dispossess, and commit acts of genocide against the people already living there.

Myth 2: "Palestinians attacked Jewish settlers out of anti-Semitism" The early Zionists' own charities show they were initially well received. Resistance began when the settlers' intent became clear, and it was no different from any other anti-colonial struggle.

The Zionist movement's own records demolish this. Early Zionist charities show settlers were well received by Palestinians, who offered them shelter. Resistance began when the settlers' intent to displace the inhabitants became clear.

That resistance was no different from any other anti-colonial struggle. Framing opposition to colonisation as bigotry denies colonised peoples the legitimacy of their response, the same tactic used against every population that resisted dispossession, from Algeria to South Africa to North America.

Myth 3: "The Palestinians are to blame for 1948: they rejected peace, left voluntarily, and lost to a tiny Israel fighting for survival" Four sub-myths collapsed by the archive: the partition plan was colonial, the exodus was forced, Israel outgunned the Arabs, and it was Israel, not the Palestinians, that rejected peace afterwards.

Four foundational fables about 1948, each collapsed by the archive:

3A: Palestinians are to blame because they rejected the UN Partition Plan. No colonised people has ever accepted partition imposed by its colonisers. The Algerians would not have accepted the partition of Algeria by French settlers. Rejecting a plan drawn up without consulting the people who lived there does not justify ethnically cleansing them as punishment.

3B: Palestinians left voluntarily. Arab leaders told them to go. Invented by Israel's foreign minister in the early 1950s. Half the refugees were expelled before May 15, 1948, before the war even began. Researcher Erskine Childers searched every 1948 Arab radio broadcast monitored by the BBC and found not a single evacuation order, only repeated broadcasts urging civilians to stay.

3C: Israel was a David fighting an Arab Goliath. The Arab states sent a small contingent of poorly equipped, poorly trained troops, outnumbered and outgunned by Zionist forces. Those troops entered Palestine after Israel declared statehood, in response to the ethnic cleansing Zionist forces had already begun in February 1948.

3D: After the war, Israel extended its hand for peace. The documents show the opposite. Israel refused to negotiate over the future of Palestine or consider the return of the expelled. Arab governments and Palestinian leaders were willing to participate in UN peace initiatives. Israel assassinated the UN peace mediator Count Bernadotte and rejected the Palestine Conciliation Commission. As Avi Shlaim showed in The Iron Wall, it was Israel that consistently rejected the peace offers on the table.

Myth 4: "Israel was a benign democracy before 1967" One-fifth of its citizens, the Palestinians, lived under military rule, denied basic rights, and were killed by Israeli security forces. It attacked its neighbours and colluded with Britain and France to topple Egypt's government.

One-fifth of Israel's citizens, the Palestinians who remained, lived under military rule, denied basic human and civil rights, with citizens killed by Israeli security forces. Israel simultaneously attacked its Arab neighbours for allowing refugees to recover lost property, and colluded with Britain and France to topple Egypt's government in 1956.

The pre-1967 democratic idyll describes a state that never existed.

Myth 5: "The Palestinian struggle is nothing but terrorism" The PLO was a liberation movement against a colonial project. The world grants legitimacy to every anti-colonial struggle, except when the oppressed are Muslim and the coloniser is a Western-backed Zionist state.

The PLO was a liberation movement against a colonial project. The world grants legitimacy to anti-colonial struggles everywhere, except when the oppressed are Muslim and the coloniser is a Western-backed Zionist state.

Israel itself recognised the PLO through Oslo. It still demonises it. The Palestinian Authority, which Israel created as a partner, is depicted as supporting terror. Hamas receives the most extreme demonisation, and mainstream media and politicians repeat it uncritically.

Myth 6: "Israel was forced to occupy the West Bank and Gaza in 1967" Israel's political and military elite regarded 1948 as a missed opportunity to take all of Palestine. Plans existed from the 1950s. In 1967, they took the chance, not because they were forced, but because they chose to.

Israel's political and military elite regarded 1948 as a missed opportunity to take all of historical Palestine. The only reason they stopped at the 1949 armistice lines was a tacit agreement with Jordan: limited Jordanian participation in the war in exchange for Jordan annexing the West Bank.

From the mid-1950s, Israel looked for a chance to take the rest. Ben-Gurion aborted plans in 1958 over fears of international reaction and in 1960 over demographics. The opportunity came in 1967. The decision to go to war was not a forced response. Seizing the West Bank and Gaza had been the plan since 1948. In 1967, Israel implemented it.

Myth 7: "Israel ran a benevolent occupation. Palestinian violence forced a tough response" From the first day, Israel punished any resistance, peaceful or armed, as terrorism. Palestinians got two options: accept life in an open-air prison, or face collective punishment.

From day one, any opposition to the occupation, peaceful or armed, was treated as terrorism, and the population was collectively punished. Palestinians got two options: accept life in an open-air prison and provide cheap labour, or resist and face house demolitions, administrative detention, and assassination.

Israel unilaterally decided which parts of the occupied territories to annex. The PLO was demonised as a terrorist organisation despite international recognition. The pattern holds to this day: armed struggle, peaceful protest, and UN votes are all treated as illegitimate.

Myth 8: "The Oslo Accords were a genuine peace process" Oslo offered the Palestinians a fragmented Bantustan on 15% of their land, with no right of return. It disempowered the PLO so completely that Arafat signed against the advice of his closest allies, and the result was the near-total destruction of Palestine.

Oslo offered Palestinians a fragmented Bantustan on 15% of their land, no right of return, and no solution for millions of refugees. The PLO was so disempowered that Arafat entered the process against his closest allies' advice, hoping to salvage independence for at least part of Palestine. The result was the near-total destruction of the Palestinian national project.

Settlement construction accelerated during and after Oslo; the settler population more than doubled. The peace process was cover for colonisation, not an alternative to it.

Myth 9: "The Second Intifada was a planned mass terror attack by Arafat" It began as a mass nonviolent protest. Israeli newspaper correspondents documented it, but editors killed the stories to fit the government narrative. The desperation came later. Israel targeted the leaders who could have led reconciliation.

The Second Intifada began as a mass nonviolent protest, triggered by Ariel Sharon's provocative visit to the al-Aqsa compound and the failure of Oslo. Israel met it with overwhelming military force, driving a more desperate Palestinian response. Israeli newspaper correspondents covered the early nonviolent phase; their editors killed the stories to fit the government's terrorism narrative.

The claim that Palestinians aborted the peace process by force is cynical. Israel was imposing its own version of Oslo by force, one designed to perpetuate the occupation, not end it. Israel systematically targeted the Palestinian leaders who could have led reconciliation, including eventually Arafat himself.

Myth 10: "A two-state solution is just around the corner" The "two-state solution" is an Israeli invention designed to keep the West Bank under Israeli control without incorporating the Palestinians. On the ground, massive colonisation has made it physically impossible, and criticism of this reality is branded antisemitism.

The West Bank is the site of a massive, ongoing colonisation project. What the "two-state solution" actually offers is a fragmented Bantustan with roughly 20% sovereignty. Gaza is excluded from most discussions. Much of Jerusalem is not included.

The "two-state solution" is an Israeli invention, designed to keep the West Bank under Israeli control without incorporating its Palestinian population. A nominally autonomous zone, perhaps called a "state," in return for Palestinians abandoning the return of refugees, equal rights, Jerusalem, and any normal life in their homeland.

Criticism of this is branded antisemitism. But it is the Zionist movement, not its critics, that fuels antisemitism, by insisting that what Israel does, it does in the name of all Jewish people. That conflation should be rejected by everyone, and especially in the name of Judaism itself. Zionism is a political project. Judaism is a faith. The actions of a settler-colonial state do not represent Jewish people any more than Saudi Arabia represents all Muslims.

The rights of everyone in Palestine, or expelled from it, must be respected. Equality for all peoples in Israel and Palestine should be the foundation of any effort for peace.

The function of mythology: These ten myths are not innocent misunderstandings. They are the intellectual infrastructure of a settler-colonial project, each one designed to neutralise a specific moral objection. "The land was empty" neutralises the objection that someone already lived there. "They left voluntarily" neutralises the objection that they were expelled. "We offered peace" neutralises the objection that we took their land by force. "It's terrorism" neutralises the objection that they have a right to resist. Stack them together and they form a closed system: every fact that challenges the project is pre-answered by a myth that makes the fact disappear. That is why dismantling them matters, not as an academic exercise, but because a peace built on mythology is no peace at all.
